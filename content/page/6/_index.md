## 22 July 2026

### [First steps forward for the implementation of the Health Action Plan](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/news/first-steps-forward-for-the-implementation-of-the-health-action-plan)

<span class="news-item__source">enisa.europa.eu</span> ENISA signed a three-year, €6 million Contribution Agreement with the European Commission to build out a "European Cybersecurity Support Centre" service catalogue for hospitals and healthcare providers (covering preparedness, detection, response, and governance), and published an updated iteration of its procurement guidelines for hospital and healthcare-provider cybersecurity, developed with the NIS Cooperation Group and the EU Health ISAC. Primary source: [ENISA news release](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/news/first-steps-forward-for-the-implementation-of-the-health-action-plan). Relevant to Maria's cybersecurity-regulation specialization and to ENISA as one of her listed target employers.

### [Commission concludes eligibility checks for two European Citizens' Initiatives on digital identity and age-verification, and on migration and asylum](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/mex_26_1669)

<span class="news-item__source">ec.europa.eu</span> The European Commission registered the European Citizens' Initiative "Stop Killing The Internet: No Digital ID & No Age Verification," finding it legally admissible under the ECI Regulation; organisers now have six months to begin collecting the one million signatures (across at least seven member states) needed within 12 months to force a Commission response. The initiative pushes back against the EU's mandatory digital-identity and age-verification push (including the age-verification app and eID rollout) on privacy and internet-freedom grounds. Primary source: [European Commission Daily News, 22 July 2026 (MEX/26/1669)](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/mex_26_1669); background at the [European Citizens' Initiative registry](https://citizens-initiative.europa.eu). Relevant to Maria's digital-rights and EU-institutions specialization, capturing organised civil-society pushback against the EU's age-verification and digital-ID agenda that her DSA/platform-regulation work intersects with.

## 21 July 2026

### [US lawmakers urge Trump to act against EU tech rules, suggest trade probes](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-lawmakers-urge-trump-act-against-eu-tech-rules-suggest-trade-probes-2026-07-21/)

<span class="news-item__source">reuters.com</span> 25 Republican US lawmakers wrote to President Trump urging Section 301 trade investigations and possible tariffs against the EU over the Digital Markets Act, criticizing likely DMA coverage of Amazon's and Microsoft's cloud units and an expected new DMA fine against Google. The European Commission rejected claims of discrimination against US firms, saying it will keep enforcing its digital rules "in a fair and non-discriminatory manner." Escalates the broader EU-US regulatory standoff over the DSA/DMA that Maria's platform-regulation profile tracks closely.

### [French lawmakers vote on social media ban for children](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/french-lawmakers-vote-social-media-ban-children-2026-07-21/)

<span class="news-item__source">reuters.com</span> French lawmakers voted on a bill to ban social media access for under-15s, following Australia's world-first under-16 ban. The law would require platforms to use age-verification systems approved by France's privacy regulator (CNIL) and give the Commission a say on whether the bill respects EU law, since Brussels is separately developing its own rules to curb addictive social media design for minors. A live, national-level test case in the same platform-regulation/minors-safety space as the EU's DSA enforcement work, directly relevant to Maria's DSA and platform-regulation profile.

### [Europe's 27 leaders to take on AI — finally](https://www.politico.eu/article/europes-27-leaders-to-take-on-ai-finally/)

<span class="news-item__source">politico.eu</span> European Council President António Costa will schedule the EU's first-ever dedicated discussion of AI among the 27 national leaders at one of the bloc's remaining 2026 summits (Oct. 15, Nov. 13 or Dec. 17), following pressure from MEPs and a 100+ expert report urging that frontier AI be elevated to the highest political level. The move reflects growing recognition that AI has outgrown ordinary digital-regulatory policy and is now a matter of security and geopolitical competitiveness, even as several EU leaders have previously pushed to delay or water down the AI Act. A major signal of where EU AI governance is headed politically, directly relevant to Maria's AI Act specialization.

### [AMLA finalises standards for supervisory cooperation in direct supervision](https://www.amla.europa.eu/press-release-amla-finalises-standards-supervisory-cooperation-direct-supervision_en)

<span class="news-item__source">amla.europa.eu</span> The EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority published final technical standards governing how it will cooperate with national supervisors to select and directly supervise the most significant cross-border financial institutions from 2028, including the data-collection process, risk-assessment methodology, and handover of supervisory history when a firm moves between national and EU-level oversight. Squarely in Maria's AML/KYC compliance specialization and relevant to Netherlands-based financial-institution compliance roles (ING, ABN AMRO, Rabobank) as AMLA's direct-supervision regime takes shape.

## 20 July 2026

### [The European Parliament's answer to its AI worries: More AI](https://www.politico.eu/article/the-european-parliaments-answer-to-its-ai-worries-more-ai/)

<span class="news-item__source">politico.eu</span> POLITICO reports that the European Parliament is rolling out an internal "EPGenAI Hub" giving MEPs and staff sanctioned access to models from Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic and Mistral, after committee chairs raised alarm over unchecked use of public AI tools (including hallucinated legal references) in drafting amendments and speeches. The rollout sits in tension with Parliament's own digital-sovereignty push, and MEPs remain under no obligation to disclose AI-assisted work even as AI Act transparency rules start to apply. Useful illustration of how an EU institution is operationalizing AI Act transparency principles internally.

### [The EU is about to sell our most sensitive data to the US for visa-free travel](https://edri.org/our-work/the-eu-is-about-to-sell-our-most-sensitive-data-to-the-us-for-visa-free-travel/)

<span class="news-item__source">edri.org</span> EDRi reports that the European Commission is finalising an "Enhanced Border Security Partnership" (EBSP) framework agreement with the Trump administration, giving US border authorities access to EU Member States' biometric databases and risk-profiling data on travellers in exchange for continued visa-free travel. A leaked draft (via Statewatch) suggests the Commission has largely caved to US demands, raising serious concerns under the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights and the "essentially equivalent protection" standard for international data transfers that the CJEU applies to such deals. Core to Maria's GDPR/international-data-transfer and human-rights specialization.

### [Commission publishes final Guidelines on AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-transparency-ai-generated-content)

<span class="news-item__source">digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu</span> Ahead of Article 50 of the AI Act entering into application on 2 August 2026, the European Commission published its final guidelines clarifying the transparency obligations for AI providers and deployers: providers must ensure people are explicitly told when interacting directly with an AI system and must add machine-readable marks to AI-generated or manipulated content, while deployers must disclose use of emotion-recognition/biometric-categorisation tools, deepfakes, and AI-generated text on matters of public interest published without human review. The guidelines complement the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content and will be enforced by national market surveillance authorities, the AI Office, and the EDPS (for EU institutions). Primary source: [Commission guidelines page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-transparency-ai-generated-content). Directly relevant to Maria's AI Act specialization and vendor/DPO-advisory work, as the compliance deadline lands in just two weeks.

### [Commission fines AliExpress €550 million for breaching the Digital Services Act](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1654)

<span class="news-item__source">ec.europa.eu</span> The European Commission fined AliExpress €550 million — the largest DSA fine to date and the third ever issued under the regulation — for failing to properly assess and mitigate the risk of illegal and unsafe products (counterfeits, unsafe toys, dangerous cosmetics) circulating on its marketplace, and for under-resourcing content moderation and recommender-system risk checks. AliExpress has until 20 October to submit a remediation plan or face further penalties, and has said it will appeal. Reported by POLITICO Europe (Mathieu Pollet) and Reuters. A landmark DSA enforcement precedent, squarely in Maria's platform-regulation/DSA specialization.
