## 19 July 2026

### [Trump pushes UN 'free speech' declaration in veiled attack on EU tech regulation](https://www.politico.eu/article/trump-administration-will-push-un-free-speech-declaration-that-takes-aim-at-european-tech-regulation/)

<span class="news-item__source">politico.eu</span> POLITICO obtained a draft US-backed "freedom of expression" declaration that the Trump administration plans to circulate for signature at the UN General Assembly in September, framing EU platform-regulation laws (chiefly the DSA) as speech-restrictive and pushing signatories to avoid obligations that could lead to "over-removal" of content. EU officials and MEPs call it a thinly veiled attack on the DSA's content-moderation and disinformation provisions. Directly relevant to Maria's DSA/platform-regulation and digital-rights expertise, and to the broader EU-US regulatory standoff.

### [EDPB calls for legal basis for cross-regulatory information sharing](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/edpb-calls-for-legal-basis-for-cross-regulatory-information-sharing_en)

<span class="news-item__source">edpb.europa.eu</span> At a high-level Dublin meeting, the EDPB called on the European Commission to propose a legal basis enabling data protection authorities to share information (including confidential information) with other EU regulators in adjacent areas of competence, citing a growing volume and complexity of GDPR complaints driven partly by AI. Relevant to Maria's GDPR-enforcement and EU-institutions specialization, illustrating the structural challenges DPAs (including a potential future employer, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens) face in policing AI-driven complaints.

## 16 July 2026

### [US judge halts sanctions policy that hit ex-EU commissioner Breton](https://www.politico.eu/article/us-judge-pauses-visa-restrictions-thierry-breton-disinformation-researchers/)

<span class="news-item__source">politico.eu</span> A US federal judge (Chief Judge James Boasberg, D.D.C.) paused a State Department policy imposing visa bans and sanctions on foreign officials and researchers involved in enforcing platform-regulation laws like the DSA, including former EU Commissioner Thierry Breton, finding the policy likely violates the First Amendment. ### [The digital rulebook is fit for purpose: better enforcement is needed, not simplification](https://edri.org/our-work/the-digital-rulebook-is-fit-for-purpose-better-enforcement-is-needed-not-simplification/)

<span class="news-item__source">edri.org</span> EDRi's submission to the European Commission's "Digital Fitness Check" consultation argues the EU's rights-based digital rulebook (GDPR, AI Act, DSA, DMA) is a competitive strength, not a burden, and that the Commission should focus on stronger enforcement rather than further "simplification" or reopening the DSA/DMA under US pressure. Calls for a Digital Fairness Act, a spyware ban, dedicated AdTech regulation, and a modernised ePrivacy framework. Useful civil-society counterpoint to the Digital Omnibus debate, relevant to Maria's EU digital-regulation and compliance-advisory profile.

### [The Digital Omnibus is going on summer break. Your rights are not.](https://edri.org/our-work/the-digital-omnibus-is-going-on-summer-break-your-rights-are-not/)

<span class="news-item__source">edri.org</span> EDRi's mid-year status check on the "Digital Omnibus": the AI Omnibus has already been adopted, delaying and weakening several AI Act safeguards, while the Data Omnibus (amending the Data Acquis, GDPR and ePrivacy rules) stalled at Council in late June after a blocking minority led by Germany and Poland, with decisions now pushed to after summer. EDRi warns the package risks narrowing the definition of personal data, expanding "legitimate interest" grounds for AI training, weakening protection of sensitive/biometric data, and shifting ePrivacy's device-access rules into looser GDPR-style exceptions. Core reading for Maria's GDPR/AI Act/ePrivacy specialization ahead of the autumn legislative fight.

### [Proposed Europol reform dangerously erodes privacy, automates surveillance, and sidelines oversight](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1420)

<span class="news-item__source">ec.europa.eu</span> The European Commission's 24 June proposal to reform Europol for the third time in six years would nearly double its budget to €3 billion, introduce automatic uploading of national police data into Europol's systems, legalise a "Europol Analytical Environment" and cloud infrastructure, lower the threshold for biometric data processing, and let Europol bypass prior EDPS approval for sensitive processing it deems "urgent." A coalition including EDRi and Access Now warns this shifts oversight from the EDPS to Europol's own internal Data Protection Officer. Directly relevant to Maria's data protection/AML-adjacent interest in law-enforcement data sharing and EDPS oversight mechanics.

### [Judgment of the Court in Case C-421/24 AGCOM (Online gambling): Google may be held liable for the YouTube videos of a content creator with whom it has a commercial partnership](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-07/cp260109en.pdf)

<span class="news-item__source">curia.europa.eu</span> The Court of Justice of the EU ruled that Google cannot claim the e-commerce/intermediary liability exemption for YouTube videos uploaded by a content creator with whom it has a commercial partnership, upholding a €750,000 Italian fine over unlawful gambling advertising. The Court held that reviewing a channel's theme, most-viewed or newest videos and associated metadata before signing a partnership deal goes beyond the "strictly technical, automated and passive" activity needed to qualify for the exemption. A landmark precedent on platform liability with direct relevance to DSA and platform-regulation practice; reported by Reuters.

### [Hadopi law (2009–2026): French Conseil d'État finds the surveillance system breaches EU fundamental rights](https://edri.org/our-work/hadopi-law-2009-2026/)

<span class="news-item__source">edri.org</span> After seven years of litigation by La Quadrature du Net, French Data Network, Franciliens.net and Fédération FDN, the Conseil d'État (French administrative supreme court) ruled that the Hadopi "graduated response" system — now operated by Arcom — breaches EU-protected fundamental rights, and ordered the government to repeal the key provisions of the underlying decree. The court held, first, that metadata retention is not organised with the "watertight separation" of IP addresses from civil-identity data that the CJEU requires (noting no legal provision imposes such retention conditions on electronic communications operators), and second, that Arcom cannot be both requester and reviewer of its own data access — though, following the CJEU, it treated the absence of independent review as fatal only from the third access onward (the registered-letter stage). In practice Arcom can no longer bring users to court. Primary source: [EDRi/La Quadrature du Net analysis of the Conseil d'État ruling](https://edri.org/our-work/hadopi-law-2009-2026/). Core material for Maria's data-retention and digital-rights expertise: a national supreme court applying CJEU metadata case law to dismantle a long-standing surveillance scheme.
