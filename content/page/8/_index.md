## 15 July 2026

### [The EU AI Act deadline moved, vendor questionnaires will not](https://iapp.org/news/a/the-eu-ai-act-deadline-moved-vendor-questionnaires-will-not)

<span class="news-item__source">iapp.org</span> IAPP analysis argues that the Digital Omnibus's delay of AI Act high-risk deadlines (to December 2027/August 2028) doesn't pause commercial reality: enterprise procurement teams will keep demanding AI Act compliance evidence from vendors regardless of the legal deadline shift, since contract renewals and vendor-risk reviews run on their own clock. Directly relevant to AI Act regulatory-affairs and vendor-compliance advisory work.

### [Commission accepts X's corrective measures to terminate breaches of the DSA](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-accepts-xs-corrective-measures-terminate-breaches-dsa)

<span class="news-item__source">digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu</span> Following a €120 million fine last year, the European Commission accepted an action plan from Elon Musk's X to fix breaches of the DSA's transparency obligations — improving its advertisement repository (search filters, API access, faster response times) and its process for granting researchers access to public data (free-of-charge, timely, no contractual scraping bans). X has six months to implement the plan under enhanced Commission supervision, after the Board for Digital Services found the original plan "overall inadequate." A useful live example of DSA enforcement mechanics and researcher-access obligations.

## 14 July 2026

### [When the facts change, adequacy must be reviewed](https://edri.org/our-work/when-the-facts-change-adequacy-must-be-reviewed/)

<span class="news-item__source">edri.org</span> EDRi and 36 other civil society organisations and academics sent an open letter to Commissioner McGrath urging the European Commission to immediately and publicly reassess the EU-US Data Privacy Framework adequacy decision, arguing that the US Supreme Court's Trump v. Slaughter ruling (allowing the president to remove independent agency commissioners) undermines the independent-oversight safeguard the adequacy finding relies on. Builds directly on the EDPB's own review of the same ruling. Core to Maria's GDPR/international-data-transfer specialization.

### [EDPB requires Belgian DPA to handle the merits of NOYB cookie banner complaint](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/edpb-requires-belgian-dpa-to-handle-the-merits-of-noyb-cookie-banner-complaint_en)

<span class="news-item__source">edpb.europa.eu</span> In a binding Article 65(1)(a) GDPR decision, the EDPB overruled the Belgian DPA's attempt (as Lead Supervisory Authority) to dismiss a NOYB cookie-banner complaint against Belgian broadcaster VRT on grounds of alleged rights abuse, siding with an Austrian DPA objection and instructing Belgium to assess the complaint on its merits. A useful worked example of the GDPR's cross-border dispute-resolution mechanism and cookie-banner enforcement, relevant to Maria's GDPR/DPA-enforcement specialization.

### ['Anthropic doesn't care about Europe' — EU officials peeved after AI giant sends junior staffer to testify about safety](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/committees/en/imco/home/highlights)

<span class="news-item__source">europarl.europa.eu</span> At a European Parliament IMCO committee hearing on cyber-capable AI models, Anthropic sent a recently hired technical employee rather than a senior policy official to answer MEPs' questions, leaving key policy questions unanswered and prompting lawmakers to say the company "doesn't care about Europe." The hearing addressed EU access to cyber-capable models (Anthropic's Mythos, OpenAI's offerings) amid concerns about critical-infrastructure risk; Anthropic said it is working with the Commission's AI Office and ENISA. A useful illustration of how AI Act-era EU institutions are engaging (and clashing) with frontier AI labs on regulatory access and accountability.

## 13 July 2026

### [US appeals court overturns Clearview AI settlement](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-appeals-court-overturns-clearview-ai-settlement-2026-07-13/)

<span class="news-item__source">reuters.com</span> The 7th US Circuit Court of Appeals threw out the 2024 class-action settlement resolving nationwide biometric-privacy claims against facial-recognition company Clearview AI, ruling that nationwide class members were inadequately represented compared to plaintiffs from Illinois, California, New York and Virginia (states with their own biometric privacy statutes). The case, over Clearview's scraping of billions of facial images without consent, returns to the district court for a possible amended settlement. Relevant to Maria's data-protection/biometrics expertise as a comparative US case study alongside GDPR-based facial-recognition enforcement in the EU.

### [EU wants 13+ age restriction for social media](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/protecting-young-people-online)

<span class="news-item__source">digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu</span> Commission President von der Leyen presented an expert panel's final report recommending that minors under 13 have only "time-limited," parent-supervised access to social media, with phased, age-appropriate access rules to follow for other platforms. The move builds on the EU's existing DSA guidelines on protection of minors and follows the Commission's preliminary Meta/Instagram addictive-design finding. Relevant to Maria's platform-regulation and DSA enforcement interests, and to Netherlands-based work on children's online safety.

## 10 July 2026

### [Commission preliminarily finds the addictive design of Instagram and Facebook in breach of the DSA](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1579)

<span class="news-item__source">ec.europa.eu</span> The European Commission preliminarily found Meta in breach of the Digital Services Act over the addictive design of Instagram and Facebook — flagging infinite scroll, autoplay, push notifications and personalised recommender systems, and finding Meta's risk assessments and mitigation measures (time-management tools, parental controls) inadequate, especially for minors. Meta can now respond before a final non-compliance decision, which could carry a fine of up to 6% of global turnover. A high-profile DSA enforcement precedent relevant to platform regulation work.

## 9 July 2026

### [Italian DPA fines Character.AI €158,000 over transparency, late DPIA and inadequate protection of minors](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/10269571)

<span class="news-item__source">garanteprivacy.it</span> The Garante per la protezione dei dati personali fined US-based Character Technologies, Inc. €158,000 (provvedimento n. 487 of 3 July 2026, announced 9 July) following an own-initiative investigation opened in November 2024 into the Character.AI generative-AI companion service. The Authority asserted jurisdiction under Article 3(2)(a) GDPR on the targeting criterion — expressly noting that this route disapplies the one-stop-shop, so every national DPA is independently competent — and found breaches of: Articles 12(1), 13 and 14 (English-only and then poorly translated privacy policies, vague retention periods, multiple alternative legal bases for the same purpose, conditional language about third-country transfers); Articles 14 and 21 (no adequate notice or objection mechanism for personal data used in LLM pre-training, rejecting the Article 14(5) "disproportionate effort" defence and reliance on blog/Reddit/Discord posts); Articles 24(1) and 25(2) (no default protections for minors before the November 2024 age gate); Articles 5(2) and 35(1) (DPIA prepared only after the regulator's first information request); and Article 27 (late appointment of the EU representative, VeraSafe Ireland, plus a broken contact link). Primary source: [Garante provvedimento n. 487/2026, doc. web 10269571](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/10269571). Exceptionally on-point for Maria's DPO/privacy-counsel target roles — it is effectively a checklist of GDPR compliance failures specific to generative-AI services, covering age assurance, training-data lawfulness and Article 27 representation.

### [Combating child sexual abuse online: support for more limited ePrivacy derogation](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20260706IPR46318/combating-child-sexual-abuse-support-for-a-more-limited-eprivacy-derogation)

<span class="news-item__source">europarl.europa.eu</span> The European Parliament adopted "Chat Control 1.0," extending the ePrivacy derogation allowing platforms to voluntarily scan private communications for CSA material until 3 April 2028, after a procedural second-reading vote fell short of the majority needed to reject it. MEPs added an amendment attempting to exclude end-to-end encrypted communications from scope, though the Council is expected to resist it. The measure now goes to Council for approval, with the more consequential "Chat Control 2.0" permanent regime still to be negotiated — a landmark case study in EU privacy vs. law-enforcement tradeoffs.
