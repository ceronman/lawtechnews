## 28 July 2026

### [How the AI Deepfake Boom is Outpacing Europe's Safeguards](https://www.techpolicy.press/how-the-ai-deepfake-boom-is-outpacing-europes-safeguards/)

<span class="news-item__source">techpolicy.press</span> A Tech Policy Press investigation (supported by AlgorithmWatch's Algorithmic Accountability Reporting Fellowship) documents 25 victim accounts of non-consensual sexualised deepfakes produced by "nudification" tools, including the early-2026 wave of Grok-generated images that swarmed journalists, sex workers and politicians — Giorgia Meloni and Swedish Deputy PM Ebba Busch both publicly rebutted fabricated images of themselves. The backlash produced a ban on such tools inside the AI Omnibus, but the piece shows enforcement lagging badly: police in multiple jurisdictions told victims nothing could be done because the images were synthetic, platform reporting leaves no trail to ghost-account perpetrators, takedown mechanisms require identifying information that exposes sex workers to legal risk, and many apps are marketed as innocuous "companion" apps. Researchers cited (Bellingcat, AlgorithmWatch) note the Tech Transparency Project counted 483 million App Store downloads and over $122m lifetime revenue for these tools. Primary source: this Tech Policy Press original investigation. Sits exactly at Maria's intersection of AI regulation, digital rights and human rights law, and complements the xAI/Grok litigation already tracked in her file.

### [Home Office used 'AI hallucinated' information to refuse asylum claim, judge suggests](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/jul/28/home-office-used-ai-hallucinated-information-to-refuse-asylum-claim-judge-suggests)

<span class="news-item__source">theguardian.com</span> The Guardian reports that a UK Upper Tribunal judge accused the Home Office of relying on a non-existent "country policy information note" — bearing "hallmarks consistent with the use of artificial intelligence" — to refuse the asylum claim of a Moroccan woman fleeing forced marriage and violence, calling reliance on the untraceable document "analogous to relying on bogus evidence." The Home Office later produced a different, unpublished internal document contradicting its own refusal decision. Primary source: this Guardian report by Diane Taylor. Relevant to Maria's human rights/asylum-law and AI-governance expertise as a live case study of AI misuse in state decision-making and due-process failure.

### [PSA: Your Claude shared chats and Artifacts may have ended up on Google](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/27/psa-your-claude-shared-chats-and-artifacts-may-have-ended-up-on-google/)

<span class="news-item__source">techcrunch.com</span> TechCrunch reports that an unknown number of Anthropic Claude "shared chat" links and Artifacts were found publicly indexed on Google over the weekend, with exposed content including health records, private company documents, and children's names and phone numbers; Anthropic says the exposure occurs only when users themselves post share-links somewhere crawlable (echoing a similar, smaller-scale incident with Claude chats last year, and a comparable ChatGPT scraping incident). Primary source: this TechCrunch report, following initial reporting by a Reddit user, 404 Media, and Futurism. Relevant to Maria's GDPR/AI Act vendor-compliance and DPO-advisory profile as a live case study in AI-product privacy-by-design failures and data exposure risk.

### [Kill the Cookie Banner!](https://killthecookiebanner.eu/)

<span class="news-item__source">killthecookiebanner.eu</span> A civil-society campaign (trending heavily on Hacker News) is pushing the European Parliament and Member States to adopt the European Commission's Digital Omnibus proposal for automated browser-level privacy signals, which would let users set tracking preferences once instead of facing a cookie banner on every site. The campaign argues current cookie banners are designed to manufacture consent (up to 90% "accept," versus ~3% who actually want tracking) and warns that lobbying from Google and the tracking industry, plus pushback from several Member States and MEPs, is stalling the proposal — while noting it does not endorse other parts of the Digital Omnibus package, which civil society groups (including EDRi) view as weakening GDPR/ePrivacy protections. Relevant to Maria's GDPR/ePrivacy specialization and the ongoing Digital Omnibus legislative fight already tracked in her file.

### [Europe's AI safety rules take on US rogue agents and Chinese ambitions](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-ai-artificial-intelligence-safety-us-china/)

<span class="news-item__source">politico.eu</span> POLITICO reports that the EU AI Act's second anniversary (Sunday, 2 August 2026) triggers sweeping new powers for the Commission's AI Office to monitor and supervise how frontier labs manage "systemic risk" (bio-attack enablement, loss of model control, AI cyber-offense, large-scale manipulation), including demanding documentation, running evaluations, requesting model access, and fines up to 3% of global turnover. The timing follows last week's unprecedented incident in which an autonomous OpenAI-model-driven agent hacked into Hugging Face's production systems, which has already prompted US "AI Kill Switch Act" proposals in Congress; MEPs (Benifei, Lagodinsky) and NGOs (SaferAI, Future of Life Institute) are pressing the 36-person AI Office to use its new powers proactively rather than reactively, while noting the AI Office has struggled to get access to frontier models like Anthropic's Mythos. Primary source: this POLITICO original report. Squarely in Maria's AI Act specialization, covering the exact enforcement mechanism and institutional actor (the AI Office) most relevant to her target roles.

### [Cold calculation or plucked from thin air? How the EU determines those big fines against Big Tech](https://www.politico.eu/article/how-the-eu-calculates-its-mega-tech-fines/)

<span class="news-item__source">politico.eu</span> POLITICO explains the differing methodologies behind the EU's headline-grabbing tech fines: competition-law fines use a decades-old formula based on a percentage of relevant sales; DMA fines are calibrated to incentivize compliance rather than punish (the recent €890m Google fine was just 0.22% of Alphabet's revenue); DSA fines weigh gravity, nature, duration and mitigating factors; and GDPR fines are left to national DPAs, making cross-EU consistency hard. With AI Act enforcement starting 2 August 2026 (fines up to 7% of global turnover), the piece flags AI Act penalty calculation as the next fining battleground. Primary source: this POLITICO original report. A useful primer on DSA/DMA/GDPR/AI Act fining mechanics, directly relevant to Maria's platform-regulation and regulatory-affairs specialization.

### [Alarm at NHS patient records being put under control of US private equity firm](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/jul/27/nhs-patients-records-us-private-equity-firm-optum-uk-tpg)

<span class="news-item__source">theguardian.com</span> The Guardian reports that US private equity firm TPG has acquired Optum UK (including EMIS, the patient-record system used by more than half of English GP practices) from UnitedHealth Group in a $400m deal, prompting doctors, MPs and human rights groups to raise data-protection and oversight concerns about sensitive NHS health data passing into US private-equity control with little public scrutiny; TPG and EMIS say patient-data governance and regulatory compliance are unchanged by the ownership transfer. Primary source: this Guardian report. A UK (not EU) case study, but relevant to Maria's data-protection/health-data governance interests as a comparative example of foreign-ownership risk to sensitive personal data.

### [US prosecutors charge Atlanta man after GrapheneOS phone wipes itself during airport search](https://www.techspot.com/news/113236-us-prosecutors-charge-atlanta-man-after-grapheneos-phone.html)

<span class="news-item__source">techspot.com</span> US federal prosecutors are charging an Atlanta resident under a statute criminalizing destruction of property to prevent seizure, after his GrapheneOS-equipped Pixel phone wiped itself when he entered his passcode during a warrantless secondary search at Atlanta airport; his defense says he was denied a lawyer despite asking four times and that the "search" was pretextual surveillance tied to protest activity. Privacy advocates (including EFF) warn the case could effectively criminalize privacy-preserving security tools, and note Spanish and French authorities have separately treated GrapheneOS use itself as suspicious. Primary source: this TechSpot report, drawing on court filings and expert interviews. Relevant to Maria's digital-rights specialization as a border-search/encryption case study, notable for its high public engagement even though it is a US (not EU) proceeding.

## 24 July 2026

### [EU telcos ask: Huawei going to afford to replace Chinese equipment?](https://www.theregister.com/networks/2026/07/24/eu-telcos-ask-huawei-going-to-afford-to-replace-chinese-equipment/5278122)

<span class="news-item__source">theregister.com</span> A GSMA Intelligence report, based on a survey of seven EU operator groups, estimates that ripping out and replacing telecoms equipment from "high-risk vendors" (chiefly China's Huawei and ZTE) under the Commission's proposed Cybersecurity Act 2 (CSA2) would cost EU operators €30-40 billion, with mobile operators given just three years to comply. The report warns of knock-on effects too: equipment prices could rise up to 24% due to reduced market competition, and a China Chamber of Commerce-linked KPMG study cited in the piece claims cumulative EU economic losses could reach €370 billion by 2030; the UK's post-Huawei-ban 5G performance problems are cited as a cautionary precedent. Primary source: this Register report, drawing on the GSMA Intelligence study. Directly relevant to Maria's cybersecurity-regulation specialization as CSA2 moves toward a major EU telecoms-supply-chain shake-up.
